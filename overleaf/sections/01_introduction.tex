\section{Introduction}

The design problem arrives before the quantum algorithm. A task in a future service organization may be assigned to a human, an AI system, or a collaborative mode. That decision simultaneously changes expected service, cost, human accountability, regional capacity, review burden, and the value of neighboring tasks. Human--AI allocation is therefore not merely personnel scheduling: it is a governance-constrained organizational design problem. Human--AI interaction research already argues for explicit authority, feedback and oversight boundaries~\citep{amershi2019humanai,seeber2020machines}; operations research supplies service and chance-constraint language~\citep{charnes1959chance,gans2003callcenters}; and sociotechnical work warns that a compact ``fairness'' scalar cannot substitute for institutional context~\citep{selbst2019fairness}.

Quantum optimization introduces a second decision: \emph{whether its evidence is worth considering at all}. Variational algorithms can concentrate probability on good combinatorial states, but small noiseless demonstrations do not establish QPU utility, runtime advantage, or scaling~\citep{preskill2018nisq,cerezo2021variational,bharti2022nisq}. A trustworthy hybrid study should accordingly use nontrivial classical controls, preserve null cases, isolate the quantum comparison on the same state set and sample budget, and classically verify any operational assignment.

We present Q-OPS, a reproducible audit that joins those two decisions. The benchmark assigns $n\in\{12,18\}$ tasks to $\{\Hmode,\Amode,\Cmode\}$ under five factor families. A coordinate-greedy incumbent is challenged by cluster destroy/repair. Their gain and disagreement help select a four-task interaction core, for which all legal assignments are classically evaluated. Uniform sampling and a warm-started feasible-subspace QAOA statevector are then compared on the \emph{same} retained state set with 128 draws. Finally, a shared classical evaluator separates evidence about sampling from acceptance of an assignment.

Our contribution is the integration and audit, not invention of its ingredients. First, we give an executable multi-factor benchmark with every coefficient and feasibility test exposed. Second, we formalize \UWCQ{} as an audit stack: uniform comparator, warm state, classical controls/certificate, and retained-state QAOA. Third, we report a frozen, null-inclusive evaluation and a reproduction audit. The headline positive signal---a 6.831-fold ratio of mean optimum masses for $p=2$ versus uniform---is reported beside three constraints on interpretation: the evidence gate is computed only \emph{after} simulation, $p=1$ often has higher optimum mass under a larger parameter search, and zero explicit mode violations does not imply composite feasibility. To our knowledge from the scoped review in \cref{sec:related}, prior work studies the components separately, but does not provide this complete reproducible combination for governed H/A/C allocation. We intentionally state this as a qualified integration claim, not an exhaustive priority claim.
